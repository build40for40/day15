# Coach reference pack

General know-how about agency projects that the coach draws on. It is industry common sense, not a rulebook: use it to spot what is missing and to explain why a question matters, never to tell people what they should agree.

## Budgets and contingency

- **What a quote covers.** A quote usually covers the agency's time to make the thing. Third-party costs (hosting, licences, stock imagery, music, fonts, app store fees, media spend, photography, talent) are often extra unless the quote lists them.
- **Fixed fee or time and materials.** A fixed fee only works with a fixed scope. Time and materials needs a cap or a regular check-in on spend.
- **Contingency.** A cushion of 10 to 20% is common for anything new or technical. Someone has to hold it, and both sides should know what it can be spent on.
- **Running costs.** Launch is not the end. Hosting, maintenance, updates for new phone software, moderation and support cost money every month.
- **Payment.** Common shapes are a deposit up front, then payments on milestones. Late payment is a quiet project killer for small agencies.
- **The cut list.** If money runs short, something gets dropped. Agreeing the order before work starts saves a fight later.

## Scope creep patterns

- **"While you're at it".** Small extras that each look free and add up to weeks.
- **New stakeholders.** Someone senior arrives late with fresh opinions, usually after designs are approved.
- **Feedback rounds.** "Two rounds" means nothing unless both sides agree what a round is, who gives the feedback and how many people's views are merged into it.
- **Moving the goalposts.** The goal changes but the budget and date do not.
- **Content.** Copy, images and data the client promised to supply arrive late or not at all, and the agency fills the gap for free.
- **Platforms.** "An app" quietly becomes iOS, Android, web and tablet.

## Procurement, legal and IP checks

- **Who owns what.** Clients often expect to own everything. Agencies often keep their tools, code libraries, engines or templates and license them. Both are normal, and they clash unless written down.
- **When ownership moves.** Usually on final payment. Until then the agency holds it.
- **Third-party rights.** Fonts, music, stock, open source code and talent each come with their own licence terms, and some expire or limit where the work can be used.
- **Supplier setup.** Big clients need the agency set up as a supplier, with insurance, security questionnaires and data protection terms. It can take weeks and nobody gets paid until it is done.
- **Data and privacy.** Anything that collects personal data needs a lawful basis and a privacy notice. Anything aimed at children needs extra care and usually parental consent.
- **Liability and termination.** What happens if it goes wrong, or if one side walks away halfway.
- **Approvals.** Regulated sectors (finance, health, alcohol, children's products) often need legal or compliance sign off on every piece of copy.

## Jargon that gets read two ways

- **Voice.** Brand tone of voice (how it sounds and writes) or a voice feature (something that talks or listens out loud). Wildly different costs.
- **AR.** A camera filter on social media, a feature inside an app, or a full augmented reality product.
- **VR.** A 360 video someone watches, or an interactive world that needs a headset.
- **AI.** A feature that calls a model, a chatbot, or "smart" in the marketing sense.
- **MVP.** The smallest thing worth launching, or a rough prototype to test an idea. One goes to customers and one does not.
- **Launch.** The day it goes live, the day it is announced, or the day it is finished.
- **Done.** Built, tested, approved, live, or handed over with training.
- **Round of feedback.** One consolidated set of comments, or every comment from everyone for a week.
- **Prototype.** Clickable pictures, or working code.
- **Responsive.** Works on phones, or designed for phones first.
- **Assets.** Design files, finished artwork, or the source code.
- **Campaign.** One piece of work, or everything for a quarter.

## The one-week sign-off rhythm

A week from pitch won to signed brief is tight but common. A rhythm that tends to work:

- **Day 1:** both sides share what they already have (pitch deck, original brief, budget line, dates).
- **Days 2 to 3:** fill the gaps: goal, audience, success measures, what the money covers, who signs off.
- **Day 4:** work through the disagreements: budget, scope, IP, feedback rounds.
- **Day 5:** procurement and legal read the agreed version, not a moving draft.
- **Last day:** both sides sign the same words.

The usual blockers are a decision maker who has not been in any conversation, procurement starting too late, and a budget line that was set before the scope was known.
